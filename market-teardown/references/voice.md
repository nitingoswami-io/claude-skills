# Voice — market teardown

## Calibration first

Before drafting, spend a minute calibrating to how the author actually writes:

1. Look for previous teardowns in the project or in past chats (search with content words from the domain, e.g. `teardown vertical SaaS construction`). If any exist, they override everything below — match them.
2. If the author has pasted their own articles, LinkedIn posts, or drafts anywhere in the conversation, match those.
3. Only if neither exists, write to the house voice below — and say once, at delivery, that the voice is calibrated to the research docs rather than to the author's own published writing, so they can correct it.

---

## The rules

**Narrative opens, data argues.** The first 150–250 words run as flowing prose and set a situation the reader can picture. But the scene is built from facts — market volumes, category shares, price gaps — not from an anecdote about a founder over coffee. The opening earns attention; every section after it earns belief, and belief comes from figures.

**Every section opens with its own conclusion.** Then supports it. Never build suspense inside a section. The reader is on a phone, and at 1,500 words they will skim at least one section — make the first line of each one carry it.

**The verdict is the destination, not a surprise.** It sits at the end, but the reader should half-know it by the third section. Sections lay track.

**Numbers do the arguing.** The default sentence contains a figure. Claims without figures get cut, not softened. Where something genuinely cannot be quantified, say so explicitly and treat the absence as a finding: *"There is no published figure for this anywhere. That is not a research failure — it is a market signal."*

**Paragraphs of three to four sentences.** Long enough to develop an idea, short enough to survive mobile. One idea per paragraph.

**First person, sparingly.** "I killed this one in week two." Not "we", not "one might argue", not "let's explore". The piece is one person's judgment with receipts.

**Concrete nouns.** Mobility heads, nursing bureaus, coaching batches, GPU-hours, storefronts, travel desks. Not "stakeholders", "solutions", "the ecosystem".

**Dry, not funny.** Understatement carries the tone: *"a profitable melting ice cube"*, *"a proven monetisation graveyard"*, *"that is validation and a clock at the same time"*, *"the lesson, stated once"*. No jokes, no exclamation marks.

**Uncertainty stated, not hedged.** Name what you don't know and what it would cost to find out.

## Banned constructions

These read as filler and undercut the analytical positioning:

- Anecdotal openers: "Last week I met a founder who..." — the opening is narrative, not personal
- Rhetorical question stacks: "What if I told you...? Sound familiar?"
- Motivational register: "the journey", "keep building", "let that sink in"
- Meta-commentary: "Here's the thing.", "Let me explain.", "But here's what nobody tells you."
- Hedge stacks: "could potentially", "it seems like it might be", "generally speaking"
- Vague magnitudes when a real number exists: "massive market", "huge opportunity", "growing rapidly"
- One-line-per-paragraph staccato formatting
- Emoji bullet points
- "In conclusion", "To sum up", "Thoughts?"

## Constructions that fit

- `Verdict: conditional go.`
- `That is validation and a clock at the same time.`
- `Which is precisely why this deserves suspicion rather than enthusiasm.`
- `A billion-dollar gap nobody has filled is usually not an oversight. It is usually a structural refusal.`
- `The lesson, stated once:`
- `Only 220k of 8.7M storefronts pay. That is the ceiling, not a growth stage.`
- `Payer identified: the aspirant, self-paid, from day one of paid beta.`
- `No payer in the loop. That is the whole finding.`
- `That counsel memo comes before the first customer pitch, not after.`

---

## The feed post is a different instrument

The teardown delivers. The feed post withholds. Same voice — dry, numeric, no motivational register — but the feed post states a contradiction and stops, naming what the teardown answers rather than answering it. Never put the verdict in the feed post.

## Density benchmark

The worked example in `references/example-teardown.md` carries roughly 30 dated figures and 15 references across ~1,450 words of body text. Anything materially thinner than that is an opinion piece wearing the format.
